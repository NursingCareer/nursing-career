🦴 01. Skeletal System

Complete system-wise nursing anatomy

Definition

The skeletal system is the supporting framework of the human body. It consists of bones, cartilage, joints and associated connective tissues.

Major Functions

Support

Maintains body shape.

Protection

Protects vital organs.

Movement

Acts as levers for muscles.

Hematopoiesis

Red marrow produces blood cells.

Mineral storage

Stores calcium and phosphate.

Fat storage

Yellow marrow stores fat.

Major Divisions

Axial Skeleton

Skull, vertebral column, ribs and sternum.

Appendicular Skeleton

Pectoral girdle, upper limbs, pelvic girdle and lower limbs.

Classification of Bones

  • Long bones — femur, humerus
  • Short bones — carpals, tarsals
  • Flat bones — sternum, ribs, many skull bones
  • Irregular bones — vertebrae
  • Sesamoid bones — patella

Important Bones

  • Skull — frontal, parietal, temporal, occipital, sphenoid, ethmoid
  • Face — maxilla, mandible, zygomatic, nasal, vomer
  • Vertebral column — cervical, thoracic, lumbar, sacral, coccygeal
  • Thorax — sternum and ribs
  • Upper limb — clavicle, scapula, humerus, radius, ulna, carpals, metacarpals, phalanges
  • Lower limb — pelvis, femur, patella, tibia, fibula, tarsals, metatarsals, phalanges

Thoracic Cage

  • 12 pairs of ribs
  • 7 pairs true ribs
  • 3 pairs false ribs
  • 2 pairs floating ribs
  • Sternum: manubrium, body, xiphoid process

Bone Structure

  • Periosteum — outer covering
  • Compact bone — dense bone
  • Spongy bone — trabecular bone
  • Medullary cavity — marrow-containing cavity
  • Epiphysis — end of long bone
  • Diaphysis — shaft
  • Metaphysis — region between shaft and end

Bone Cells

  • Osteoblast — forms bone
  • Osteocyte — mature bone cell
  • Osteoclast — bone resorption

Joints

  • Fibrous joints
  • Cartilaginous joints
  • Synovial joints

Synovial joint examples

  • Ball-and-socket — shoulder, hip
  • Hinge — elbow, knee
  • Pivot — atlantoaxial joint
  • Saddle — thumb joint
  • Plane — some intercarpal joints

📝 HAND NOTE

  • Adult skeleton → 206 bones
  • Longest bone → Femur
  • Smallest bone → Stapes
  • C1 → Atlas
  • C2 → Axis
  • Bone-forming → Osteoblast
  • Bone-resorbing → Osteoclast
  • Muscle to bone → Tendon
  • Bone to bone → Ligament

🎯 HIGH-YIELD

  • Femur is the longest bone.
  • Patella is a sesamoid bone.
  • True ribs are 1–7.
  • Floating ribs are 11–12.
  • Tibia is the major weight-bearing bone of the leg.

🩺 CLINICAL CONNECTION

  • Fracture → break in bone continuity.
  • Dislocation → loss of normal joint relationship.
  • Osteoporosis → decreased bone density.
  • Osteomyelitis → infection of bone.
  • Fracture assessment includes distal circulation and neurological status.

💪 02. Muscular System

Definition

The muscular system consists of specialized contractile tissues responsible for movement, posture, heat production and movement of substances within the body.

Types of Muscle

Skeletal Muscle

Striated, voluntary muscle attached mainly to bones.

Cardiac Muscle

Striated, involuntary muscle of heart.

Smooth Muscle

Non-striated, involuntary muscle in hollow organs.

Functions

  • Movement
  • Maintenance of posture
  • Joint stabilization
  • Heat production
  • Movement of food through GI tract
  • Blood vessel diameter regulation
  • Cardiac pumping

Major Muscles

  • Masseter — chewing
  • Sternocleidomastoid — neck movement
  • Trapezius — shoulder/neck movement
  • Deltoid — arm abduction
  • Biceps brachii — elbow flexion
  • Triceps brachii — elbow extension
  • Pectoralis major — chest/upper limb movement
  • Rectus abdominis — trunk flexion
  • Gluteus maximus — hip extension
  • Quadriceps — knee extension
  • Hamstrings — knee flexion
  • Gastrocnemius — plantar flexion

Muscle Attachments

A tendon connects muscle to bone. Fascia surrounds and separates muscles and other structures.

📝 HAND NOTE

  • Skeletal → voluntary
  • Cardiac → heart
  • Smooth → hollow organs
  • Biceps → flexion
  • Triceps → extension
  • Quadriceps → knee extension
  • Hamstrings → knee flexion

🎯 HIGH-YIELD

  • Cardiac muscle is found in myocardium.
  • Skeletal muscle is mainly voluntary.
  • Smooth muscle is found in intestine and blood vessels.
  • Muscle contraction produces movement and heat.

🩺 CLINICAL

  • Muscle strain involves overstretching or tearing of muscle/tendon.
  • Muscle weakness can affect mobility and activities of daily living.
  • Prolonged immobility can cause muscle wasting.

✍️ SHORT QUESTIONS

❤️ 03. Cardiovascular System

Components

The cardiovascular system consists of the heart, blood and blood vessels.

Heart Location

The heart lies in the thoracic cavity within the mediastinum, between the lungs.

Heart Chambers

Right Atrium

Receives systemic venous blood.

Right Ventricle

Pumps blood toward lungs.

Left Atrium

Receives oxygenated pulmonary blood.

Left Ventricle

Pumps blood to systemic circulation.

Heart Valves

  • Tricuspid valve — right atrioventricular valve
  • Mitral/bicuspid valve — left atrioventricular valve
  • Pulmonary valve
  • Aortic valve

Major Blood Vessels

  • Superior vena cava
  • Inferior vena cava
  • Pulmonary trunk/arteries
  • Pulmonary veins
  • Aorta

Circulation

  • Pulmonary circulation — heart to lungs and back.
  • Systemic circulation — heart to body and back.
  • Coronary circulation — supplies the heart muscle.

Blood Vessels

  • Arteries — carry blood away from heart.
  • Veins — carry blood toward heart.
  • Capillaries — exchange vessels between arterial and venous sides.

📝 HAND NOTE

  • Right side → mainly deoxygenated blood
  • Left side → mainly oxygenated blood
  • Tricuspid → right AV valve
  • Mitral → left AV valve
  • Aorta → systemic arterial supply
  • Pulmonary artery → carries blood from heart to lungs
  • Pulmonary veins → return blood from lungs to left atrium

🎯 HIGH-YIELD

  • Heart has four chambers.
  • Left ventricle pumps into aorta.
  • Right ventricle pumps into pulmonary trunk.
  • Coronary arteries supply myocardium.

🩺 CLINICAL

  • Myocardial infarction results from interruption of coronary blood supply.
  • Heart failure can cause pulmonary and/or systemic congestion.
  • Peripheral pulse assessment gives information about arterial circulation.

🫁 04. Respiratory System

Main Parts

  • Nose and nasal cavity
  • Pharynx
  • Larynx
  • Trachea
  • Bronchi
  • Bronchioles
  • Lungs
  • Alveoli

Air Passage

Air normally passes through the nose → pharynx → larynx → trachea → bronchi → bronchioles → alveoli.

Lungs

The right lung normally has three lobes, while the left lung has two lobes and a cardiac impression.

Alveoli

Alveoli are microscopic air spaces where gas exchange occurs between air and pulmonary capillary blood.

Pleura

  • Visceral pleura covers lung surface.
  • Parietal pleura lines the thoracic cavity.
  • Pleural cavity lies between them.

Functions

  • Oxygen intake
  • Carbon dioxide removal
  • Acid-base contribution
  • Voice production through larynx
  • Air conditioning and filtration

📝 HAND NOTE

  • Gas exchange → alveoli
  • Right lung → 3 lobes
  • Left lung → 2 lobes
  • Windpipe → trachea
  • Voice box → larynx
  • Airway branches → bronchi → bronchioles

🎯 HIGH-YIELD

  • Alveoli are the principal gas-exchange units.
  • Right lung has three lobes.
  • Left lung has two lobes.
  • Diaphragm is the major muscle of quiet inspiration.

🩺 CLINICAL

  • Pneumonia affects lung tissue and can impair gas exchange.
  • Asthma causes variable airway obstruction.
  • Pneumothorax involves air in the pleural space.
  • Hypoxemia means low oxygen level in arterial blood.

✍️ SHORT QUESTIONS

Alveoli.

Three.

Two.
Main muscle of inspiration?
Diaphragm.
Gas exchange mainly occurs in:
A. Trachea
B. Bronchus
C. Alveoli
D. Larynx

🧠 05. Nervous System

Divisions

  • Central nervous system — brain and spinal cord
  • Peripheral nervous system — nerves outside CNS

Brain

  • Cerebrum
  • Diencephalon
  • Brainstem
  • Cerebellum

Cerebrum

The cerebrum is the largest part of the brain and is involved in conscious perception, voluntary movement, memory, language and higher cognitive functions.

Cerebellum

The cerebellum contributes to coordination, balance and fine control of movement.

Brainstem

  • Midbrain
  • Pons
  • Medulla oblongata

Spinal Cord

The spinal cord lies within the vertebral canal and provides pathways between brain and body while participating in reflexes.

Meninges

  • Dura mater
  • Arachnoid mater
  • Pia mater

Autonomic Nervous System

  • Sympathetic division
  • Parasympathetic division

📝 HAND NOTE

  • CNS → brain + spinal cord
  • PNS → peripheral nerves
  • Cerebrum → higher functions
  • Cerebellum → coordination and balance
  • Medulla → important autonomic functions
  • Meninges → dura, arachnoid, pia

🎯 HIGH-YIELD

  • Largest brain part → cerebrum.
  • Coordination → cerebellum.
  • Brainstem includes midbrain, pons and medulla.
  • Spinal cord is protected by vertebral column.

🩺 CLINICAL

  • Stroke can produce focal neurological deficits.
  • Spinal cord injury can cause sensory and motor impairment.
  • Meningitis involves inflammation of the meninges.
  • Raised intracranial pressure requires urgent assessment.

✍️ SHORT QUESTIONS

Dura, arachnoid and pia mater.

🍽️ 06. Digestive System

Alimentary Canal

The digestive tract is a continuous muscular tube extending from mouth to anus.

  • Mouth
  • Pharynx
  • Esophagus
  • Stomach
  • Small intestine
  • Large intestine
  • Rectum
  • Anal canal

Small Intestine

  • Duodenum
  • Jejunum
  • Ileum

It is the major site of digestion and nutrient absorption.

Large Intestine

  • Cecum
  • Colon
  • Rectum
  • Anal canal

It absorbs water and electrolytes and forms/stores feces.

Stomach

  • Cardia
  • Fundus
  • Body
  • Pyloric part

Liver

The liver is a major metabolic organ and produces bile. It also participates in nutrient processing, detoxification, protein synthesis and storage.

Gallbladder

The gallbladder stores and concentrates bile.

Pancreas

The pancreas has exocrine digestive functions and endocrine functions related to glucose regulation.

📝 HAND NOTE

  • Food pathway → mouth → pharynx → esophagus → stomach → small intestine → large intestine → rectum → anal canal
  • Small intestine → duodenum + jejunum + ileum
  • Liver → produces bile
  • Gallbladder → stores bile
  • Pancreas → digestive enzymes + endocrine hormones

🎯 HIGH-YIELD

  • Major nutrient absorption → small intestine.
  • Water absorption is an important function of large intestine.
  • Bile is produced by liver.
  • Bile is stored in gallbladder.

🩺 CLINICAL

  • Appendicitis involves inflammation of appendix.
  • Peptic ulcer can affect stomach or duodenum.
  • Hepatitis affects liver.
  • Gallstones can obstruct biliary pathways.

🚽 07. Urinary System

Main Organs

  • Two kidneys
  • Two ureters
  • Urinary bladder
  • Urethra

Kidney

Kidneys are paired organs located posteriorly in the abdomen. They regulate fluid, electrolyte and acid-base balance and remove metabolic waste through urine formation.

Nephron

  • Renal corpuscle
  • Glomerulus
  • Bowman's capsule
  • Proximal convoluted tubule
  • Loop of Henle
  • Distal convoluted tubule
  • Collecting duct

Functions of Kidney

  • Urine formation
  • Waste excretion
  • Fluid balance
  • Electrolyte balance
  • Acid-base regulation
  • Blood pressure regulation
  • Erythropoietin production
  • Vitamin D activation

Urine Pathway

Kidney → ureter → urinary bladder → urethra → outside.

📝 HAND NOTE

  • Functional unit → nephron
  • Filtration begins at glomerulus
  • Ureter carries urine to bladder
  • Bladder stores urine
  • Urethra carries urine outside

🎯 HIGH-YIELD

  • Kidney is the major organ of urine formation.
  • Nephron is the functional unit.
  • Glomerulus is a capillary network involved in filtration.
  • Ureter is not the same as urethra.

🩺 CLINICAL

  • Urinary tract infection may affect bladder or other urinary structures.
  • Kidney stones can obstruct urinary flow.
  • Acute kidney injury may reduce urine production.
  • Urine output is an important nursing assessment parameter.

✍️ SHORT QUESTIONS

Nephron.

Ureter.

Urethra.

🦋 08. Endocrine System

Definition

The endocrine system consists of glands that release hormones into the bloodstream to regulate body functions.

Major Endocrine Glands

Pituitary

Located at base of brain; regulates several endocrine functions.

Thyroid

Neck; thyroid hormones regulate metabolism.

Parathyroids

Regulate calcium homeostasis.

Adrenals

Located above kidneys.

Pancreatic Islets

Insulin and glucagon.

Pineal

Associated with melatonin secretion.

Important Hormones

  • Insulin — lowers blood glucose.
  • Glucagon — raises blood glucose.
  • Thyroid hormones — regulate metabolic activity.
  • Parathyroid hormone — increases blood calcium.
  • Adrenaline — participates in stress response.
  • Cortisol — important in stress and metabolism.

📝 HAND NOTE

  • Thyroid → neck
  • Parathyroid → posterior thyroid region
  • Adrenal → superior to kidneys
  • Insulin → beta cells of pancreatic islets
  • Glucagon → alpha cells of pancreatic islets

🎯 HIGH-YIELD

  • Pituitary is commonly called the master gland in basic textbooks.
  • Thyroid is located in the neck.
  • Parathyroid hormone regulates calcium.
  • Insulin decreases blood glucose.

🩺 CLINICAL

  • Diabetes mellitus involves abnormal glucose regulation.
  • Hypothyroidism is associated with reduced thyroid hormone effect.
  • Hyperthyroidism is associated with increased thyroid hormone effect.
  • Adrenal disorders can affect blood pressure and metabolism.

✍️ SHORT QUESTIONS

Thyroid gland.
Which hormone lowers glucose?
Insulin.
Which hormone raises glucose?
Glucagon.
Insulin is produced by:
A. Thyroid
B. Pancreatic beta cells
C. Adrenal cortex
D. Pituitary
✓ Answer: B

👩 09. Female Reproductive System

Main Organs

  • Ovaries
  • Uterine tubes
  • Uterus
  • Cervix
  • Vagina
  • External genitalia

Ovary

The ovaries are female gonads. They produce ova and reproductive hormones including estrogen and progesterone.

Uterine Tube

The uterine tube transports the oocyte toward the uterus. The ampulla is a common site of fertilization.

Uterus

  • Fundus
  • Body
  • Cervix

Layers

  • Perimetrium
  • Myometrium
  • Endometrium

Vagina

The vagina is a muscular canal extending from the cervix to the external genital region and serves as the birth canal.

📝 HAND NOTE

  • Female gonad → ovary
  • Common site of fertilization → ampulla of uterine tube
  • Uterus layers → perimetrium, myometrium, endometrium
  • Myometrium → smooth muscle layer
  • Endometrium → inner mucosal layer
  • Vagina → birth canal

🎯 HIGH-YIELD

  • Ovary produces ova and hormones.
  • Fertilization commonly occurs in ampulla.
  • Myometrium is important for uterine contraction.
  • Endometrium undergoes cyclic changes.

🩺 CLINICAL

  • Ectopic pregnancy can occur when implantation occurs outside the uterine cavity, commonly in a uterine tube.
  • Fibroids are benign smooth-muscle tumors of the uterus.
  • Cervical screening helps detect cervical abnormalities.

👨 10. Male Reproductive System

Main Organs

  • Testes
  • Epididymis
  • Vas deferens
  • Seminal vesicles
  • Prostate gland
  • Penis
  • Scrotum

Testis

The testes are male gonads. They produce sperm and testosterone. Sperm develop within seminiferous tubules.

Epididymis

The epididymis is a coiled duct associated with sperm maturation and storage.

Vas Deferens

The vas deferens transports sperm from the epididymis toward the ejaculatory duct.

Accessory Glands

  • Seminal vesicles
  • Prostate gland
  • Bulbourethral glands

📝 HAND NOTE

  • Male gonad → testis
  • Sperm production → seminiferous tubules
  • Sperm maturation/storage → epididymis
  • Sperm transport → vas deferens
  • Important accessory glands → seminal vesicles, prostate, bulbourethral glands

🎯 HIGH-YIELD

  • Testosterone is produced mainly by Leydig cells.
  • Sperm are produced in seminiferous tubules.
  • Epididymis is important for sperm maturation.
  • Prostate surrounds the proximal urethra.

🩺 CLINICAL

  • Benign prostatic enlargement can obstruct urinary flow.
  • Testicular torsion is a surgical emergency.
  • Infections can affect epididymis and testes.

🛡️ 11. Lymphatic System

Components

  • Lymph
  • Lymphatic vessels
  • Lymph nodes
  • Spleen
  • Thymus
  • Tonsils

Functions

  • Returns excess tissue fluid to circulation.
  • Participates in immune defense.
  • Transports absorbed dietary fats through specialized lymphatic vessels.

Lymph Nodes

Lymph nodes are small immune organs located along lymphatic vessels. They filter lymph and provide sites for immune-cell activity.

Spleen

The spleen is a major lymphoid organ. It participates in immune responses and blood filtration.

Thymus

The thymus is important for T-lymphocyte maturation, especially during childhood.

📝 HAND NOTE

  • Lymph nodes → filter lymph
  • Spleen → lymphoid organ and blood filtration
  • Thymus → T-cell maturation
  • Lymphatic system → immunity + fluid return

🎯 HIGH-YIELD

  • Spleen is the largest lymphoid organ.
  • Thymus is important for T-cell maturation.
  • Lymph nodes filter lymph.
  • Lymphatic vessels return tissue fluid to circulation.

🩺 CLINICAL

  • Lymph node enlargement may occur during infection.
  • Lymphedema results from impaired lymph drainage.
  • Sentinel lymph node assessment is relevant in some cancers.

✍️ SHORT QUESTIONS

Lymph nodes.

👁️ 12. Special Senses

Major Special Senses

  • Vision
  • Hearing
  • Balance
  • Smell
  • Taste

Eye

  • Cornea
  • Sclera
  • Iris
  • Pupil
  • Lens
  • Retina
  • Optic nerve

Retina

The retina contains photoreceptors and neural elements involved in vision. Rods are associated mainly with low-light vision and cones with color and high-acuity vision.

Ear

  • External ear
  • Middle ear
  • Inner ear

Important Structures

  • Tympanic membrane
  • Auditory ossicles
  • Cochlea
  • Semicircular canals
  • Vestibular structures

Hearing & Balance

The cochlea is mainly concerned with hearing. The vestibular apparatus contributes to balance and spatial orientation.

📝 HAND NOTE

  • Vision → eye
  • Photoreceptors → retina
  • Hearing → cochlea
  • Balance → vestibular apparatus
  • Light enters through → pupil
  • Focus → lens

🎯 HIGH-YIELD

  • Retina contains rods and cones.
  • Cochlea is important for hearing.
  • Semicircular canals participate in balance.
  • Optic nerve carries visual information from eye.

🩺 CLINICAL

  • Cataract involves opacity of the lens.
  • Glaucoma involves optic nerve damage associated with abnormal intraocular pressure.
  • Otitis media affects the middle ear.
  • Hearing loss can arise from conductive or sensorineural mechanisms.

🧴 13. Integumentary System

Components

  • Skin
  • Hair
  • Nails
  • Sweat glands
  • Sebaceous glands

Layers of Skin

Epidermis

The outer epithelial layer of skin. It forms a protective barrier.

Dermis

The connective tissue layer beneath the epidermis containing blood vessels, nerves, glands and hair follicles.

Subcutaneous Tissue

Located beneath the skin; contains variable amounts of adipose and connective tissue.

Functions of Skin

  • Protection
  • Temperature regulation
  • Sensation
  • Prevention of excessive fluid loss
  • Vitamin D-related metabolic function
  • Immune defense

Skin Appendages

  • Hair follicles
  • Nails
  • Sweat glands
  • Sebaceous glands

Sweat & Sebaceous Glands

Sweat glands participate in thermoregulation. Sebaceous glands produce sebum, which helps lubricate skin and hair.

📝 HAND NOTE

  • Outer layer → Epidermis
  • Deeper connective layer → Dermis
  • Below skin → Subcutaneous tissue
  • Sweat → thermoregulation
  • Sebaceous gland → sebum
  • Skin → protection + sensation

🎯 HIGH-YIELD

  • Epidermis is the outer layer.
  • Dermis contains vessels, nerves and glands.
  • Sweating helps regulate body temperature.
  • Sebaceous glands produce sebum.

🩺 CLINICAL

  • Pressure injuries commonly develop over bony prominences.
  • Burns can damage different depths of skin and underlying tissue.
  • Skin assessment is important in nursing care.
  • Dehydration may cause dry skin and reduced skin turgor.
