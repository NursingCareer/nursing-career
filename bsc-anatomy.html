📖 Chapter 01 — Skeletal System

Complete textbook-style study notes

1. What is the Skeletal System?

The skeletal system is the structural framework of the human body. It consists mainly of bones, cartilage, joints and associated connective tissues.

The skeleton provides support to the body, protects vital organs, participates in movement and serves as an important site for mineral storage and blood-cell formation.

2. Functions of the Skeletal System

1. Support

Bones form the framework that supports the body and maintains its shape.

2. Protection

Skull protects the brain, vertebral column protects the spinal cord, and thoracic cage protects heart and lungs.

3. Movement

Bones act as levers and muscles provide the force required for movement.

4. Blood Cell Formation

Red bone marrow is an important site of hematopoiesis.

5. Mineral Storage

Bone stores important minerals, especially calcium and phosphate.

6. Fat Storage

Yellow bone marrow stores fat.

3. Number of Bones

An adult human skeleton normally contains approximately 206 bones.

Newborns have more individual bones because several bones fuse together during growth.

4. Divisions of the Skeleton

Axial Skeleton

The axial skeleton forms the central axis of the body.

  • Skull
  • Vertebral column
  • Ribs
  • Sternum

Appendicular Skeleton

The appendicular skeleton includes the limbs and the girdles that attach the limbs to the axial skeleton.

  • Pectoral girdle
  • Upper limbs
  • Pelvic girdle
  • Lower limbs

5. Classification of Bones

Long Bones

Femur, humerus, tibia, radius.

Short Bones

Carpal and tarsal bones.

Flat Bones

Some skull bones, sternum and ribs.

Irregular Bones

Vertebrae and some facial bones.

Sesamoid Bones

Patella is the major example.

6. Structure of a Long Bone

Epiphysis

The expanded end of a long bone. It mainly contains spongy bone and is involved in joint formation.

Diaphysis

The shaft or central portion of a long bone. It is mainly composed of compact bone surrounding the medullary cavity.

Metaphysis

The region between the diaphysis and epiphysis. In growing children, the epiphyseal plate is located in this region.

Periosteum

A vascular connective tissue membrane covering the external surface of bone except at articular surfaces.

Compact Bone

Dense bone forming the strong outer portion of many bones.

Spongy Bone

Also called cancellous bone. It contains a network of trabeculae.

Medullary Cavity

The cavity within the shaft of a long bone containing marrow.

📝 HAND NOTE — Long Bone

  • End → Epiphysis
  • Shaft → Diaphysis
  • Between them → Metaphysis
  • Outer covering → Periosteum
  • Dense part → Compact bone
  • Trabecular part → Spongy bone
  • Inside shaft → Medullary cavity

7. Skull — Detailed Anatomy

The skull forms the bony framework of the head. It protects the brain and supports the structures of the face.

Major Divisions

  • Cranium
  • Facial skeleton

Important Cranial Bones

  • Frontal bone
  • Parietal bones
  • Temporal bones
  • Occipital bone
  • Sphenoid bone
  • Ethmoid bone

Important Facial Bones

  • Maxilla
  • Mandible
  • Zygomatic bones
  • Nasal bones
  • Lacrimal bones
  • Palatine bones
  • Vomer
  • Inferior nasal conchae

8. Important Skull Facts

  • Frontal bone forms much of the forehead.
  • Parietal bones form a large part of the roof and sides of skull.
  • Temporal bones are located on the sides of the skull.
  • Occipital bone forms the posterior part of skull.
  • Mandible is the movable bone of the lower jaw.
  • Maxilla forms much of the upper jaw.

🎯 HIGH-YIELD — Skull

  • Mandible → major movable facial bone.
  • Occipital bone contains the foramen magnum.
  • Temporal bone is associated with structures of the ear.
  • Frontal bone → forehead.
  • Parietal bones → roof and sides of skull.

9. Vertebral Column

The vertebral column forms the central supporting axis of the body and protects the spinal cord.

Regions

Cervical

7 vertebrae

Thoracic

12 vertebrae

Lumbar

5 vertebrae

Sacral

Fused vertebrae

Coccygeal

Usually fused vertebrae

10. Special Vertebrae

Atlas — C1

The first cervical vertebra. It supports the skull and participates in nodding movement of the head.

Axis — C2

The second cervical vertebra. It contains the dens, which allows rotation of the head.

Cervical Vertebrae

There are seven cervical vertebrae.

Thoracic Vertebrae

There are twelve thoracic vertebrae and they articulate with ribs.

Lumbar Vertebrae

Five lumbar vertebrae are present and they are relatively large because they bear substantial body weight.

📝 HAND NOTE — Vertebral Column

  • Cervical → 7
  • Thoracic → 12
  • Lumbar → 5
  • C1 → Atlas
  • C2 → Axis
  • Axis has → dens
  • Thoracic vertebrae → articulate with ribs

11. Thoracic Cage

The thoracic cage protects the heart and lungs and participates in respiration.

Main Components

  • Sternum
  • 12 pairs of ribs
  • Thoracic vertebrae

Parts of Sternum

  • Manubrium
  • Body
  • Xiphoid process

12. Classification of Ribs

True Ribs

First seven pairs. They attach directly to the sternum through their costal cartilages.

False Ribs

Ribs 8–10 attach indirectly to the sternum.

Floating Ribs

Ribs 11–12 have no anterior attachment to the sternum.

🎯 HIGH-YIELD — Thoracic Cage

  • 12 pairs of ribs.
  • Ribs 1–7 → true ribs.
  • Ribs 8–10 → false ribs.
  • Ribs 11–12 → floating ribs.
  • Sternum → manubrium + body + xiphoid process.

13. Upper Limb Bones

Pectoral Girdle

  • Clavicle
  • Scapula

Arm

The arm contains the humerus.

Forearm

  • Radius
  • Ulna

Hand

  • 8 carpal bones
  • 5 metacarpals
  • 14 phalanges

14. Important Upper Limb Facts

  • Humerus → bone of arm.
  • Radius → lateral forearm in anatomical position.
  • Ulna → medial forearm in anatomical position.
  • Carpals → wrist.
  • Metacarpals → palm.
  • Phalanges → fingers.

📝 HAND NOTE — Upper Limb

  • Shoulder girdle → clavicle + scapula
  • Arm → humerus
  • Forearm → radius + ulna
  • Wrist → 8 carpals
  • Palm → 5 metacarpals
  • Digits → 14 phalanges

15. Lower Limb Bones

Pelvic Girdle

The hip bone is formed by fusion of:

  • Ilium
  • Ischium
  • Pubis

Thigh

Femur is the bone of the thigh.

Leg

  • Tibia
  • Fibula

Foot

  • 7 tarsal bones
  • 5 metatarsals
  • 14 phalanges

16. Important Lower Limb Facts

  • Femur → thigh.
  • Patella → knee.
  • Tibia → main weight-bearing bone of leg.
  • Fibula → lateral bone of leg.
  • Tarsals → ankle/foot region.
  • Metatarsals → foot.
  • Phalanges → toes.

🎯 HIGH-YIELD — Lower Limb

  • Femur is the longest bone in the body.
  • Patella is a sesamoid bone.
  • Tibia is the larger medial bone of leg.
  • Fibula is the slender lateral bone of leg.

17. Joints

A joint is the anatomical connection between two or more bones or between bone and cartilage.

Major Types

  • Fibrous joints
  • Cartilaginous joints
  • Synovial joints

18. Synovial Joints

Synovial joints are freely movable joints with a joint cavity.

Types

  • Ball-and-socket → shoulder, hip
  • Hinge → elbow, knee
  • Pivot → atlantoaxial joint
  • Condyloid → wrist region
  • Saddle → thumb carpometacarpal joint
  • Plane → some intercarpal joints

19. Cartilage

Cartilage is a specialized connective tissue that provides support, flexibility and smooth surfaces at many joints.

  • Hyaline cartilage
  • Elastic cartilage
  • Fibrocartilage

20. Bone Cells

Osteoblast

Bone-forming cell.

Osteocyte

Mature bone cell.

Osteoclast

Cell involved in bone resorption.

📝 HAND NOTE — Bone Cells

  • Osteoblast → builds bone
  • Osteocyte → mature bone cell
  • Osteoclast → resorbs bone

21. Blood Supply of Bone

Bones are vascular structures. Nutrient arteries supply the bone, while periosteal vessels contribute to the outer portions.

Long bones commonly receive a nutrient artery that enters through a nutrient foramen and supplies the internal bone and marrow.

22. Bone Marrow

Red Bone Marrow

Red marrow is associated with hematopoiesis or blood cell production.

Yellow Bone Marrow

Yellow marrow contains a greater amount of fat and serves as an energy reserve.

23. Fracture

A fracture is a break or disruption in the continuity of a bone.

Common Types

  • Closed fracture
  • Open fracture
  • Greenstick fracture
  • Comminuted fracture
  • Transverse fracture
  • Oblique fracture
  • Spiral fracture

🩺 CLINICAL ANATOMY — Nursing Connection

  • Fracture: Observe pain, swelling, deformity and neurovascular status.
  • Osteoporosis: Reduced bone density increases fracture risk.
  • Osteomyelitis: Bone infection requiring appropriate assessment and treatment.
  • Dislocation: Normal relationship between joint surfaces is disturbed.
  • Joint injury: Assessment should include movement, pain, swelling and distal circulation.

✍️ SHORT QUESTIONS & ANSWERS

1. What is the skeletal system?
Ans: The structural framework of the body consisting mainly of bones, cartilage and joints.
2. How many bones are present in an adult skeleton?
Ans: Approximately 206.
3. What is the longest bone?
Ans: Femur.
4. What is the smallest bone?
Ans: Stapes.
5. What is a sesamoid bone?
Ans: A bone developing within a tendon; patella is a major example.
6. Name the two divisions of skeleton.
Ans: Axial and appendicular skeleton.
7. How many cervical vertebrae are present?
Ans: Seven.
8. How many thoracic vertebrae are present?
Ans: Twelve.
9. How many lumbar vertebrae are present?
Ans: Five.
10. What is C1 called?
Ans: Atlas.
11. What is C2 called?
Ans: Axis.
12. What is the dens?
Ans: A prominent process of the axis that participates in rotation of the atlas and head.
13. How many pairs of ribs are present?
Ans: Twelve pairs.
14. What are true ribs?
Ans: Ribs 1–7, which attach directly to the sternum through their costal cartilage.
15. What are floating ribs?
Ans: Ribs 11–12, which have no anterior attachment to the sternum.
16. What connects muscle to bone?
Ans: Tendon.
17. What connects bone to bone?
Ans: Ligament.
18. What is the functional unit of compact bone?
Ans: Osteon.
19. What is the bone-forming cell?
Ans: Osteoblast.
20. What is the bone-resorbing cell?
Ans: Osteoclast.
