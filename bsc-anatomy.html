LEVEL 1 — BASIC ANATOMY

  • Definition: The skeletal system is the framework of bones, cartilages, and joints that supports and protects the body and enables movement.
  • Location: Whole body; organized into axial (skull, vertebral column, ribs, sternum) and appendicular (pectoral and pelvic girdles, upper and lower limbs).
  • Main parts: 206 bones in adult; bone tissue (compact, spongy), bone marrow (red/yellow), periosteum (outer), endosteum (inner).
  • Basic structure: Long bones (diaphysis, metaphysis, epiphysis), short, flat, irregular, sesamoid bones.
  • Function: Support, protection, movement (with muscles), mineral storage (Ca, P), hematopoiesis (red marrow).
  • Easy concepts: 7 cervical, 12 thoracic, 5 lumbar vertebrae; 12 pairs of ribs; right vs left bones by features.
  • Terminology: Proximal/distal, medial/lateral, superior/inferior, anterior/posterior, synarthrosis/amphiarthrosis/diarthrosis.

LEVEL 2 — DETAILED ANATOMY

  • Subdivisions: Axial skeleton (skull, vertebral column incl. sacrum/coccyx, thoracic cage); Appendicular skeleton (limb girdles and limbs).
  • Skull: Cranial bones (frontal, parietal, temporal, occipital, sphenoid, ethmoid); Facial bones (maxillae, zygomatics, nasal, lacrimal, palatine, inferior conchae, mandible, vomer).
  • Vertebral column: Typical vertebra anatomy (body, arch, pedicles, laminae, spinous/transverse processes). Cervical have transverse foramina; thoracic have costal facets; lumbar have large bodies.
  • Thoracic cage: Sternum (manubrium, body, xiphoid) and ribs (true 1–7, false 8–10, floating 11–12).
  • Pectoral girdle: Clavicle and scapula; Glenohumeral joint is a ball-and-socket synovial joint.
  • Pelvic girdle: Hip bone (ilium, ischium, pubis) and sacrum; acetabulum articulates with femoral head.
  • Long bone blood supply: Nutrient artery via nutrient foramen to medullary cavity; metaphyseal/epiphyseal arteries; periosteal vessels supply compact bone.
  • Nerve supply: Periosteum richly innervated (pain); nutrient nerves accompany arteries.
  • Joints: Fibrous, cartilaginous (primary—synchondrosis; secondary—symphysis), and synovial (plane, hinge, pivot, condylar, saddle, ball-and-socket).
  • Lymphatic drainage: Along periosteal vessels to regional nodes (e.g., humerus to axillary lymph nodes).
  • Landmarks: External auditory meatus to inferior orbital margin (Frankfort plane), sternal angle at 2nd rib, iliac crest level L4.

LEVEL 3 — CLINICAL ANATOMY

  • Fractures: Colles fracture (distal radius), scaphoid fracture (risk of avascular necrosis), femoral neck fractures (risk to medial circumflex femoral artery).
  • Spine: Disc herniation most commonly posterolateral at L4–L5 or L5–S1 compressing nerve roots.
  • Osteoporosis: Vertebral wedge fractures; nursing—fall risk prevention.
  • Osteomyelitis: Infection of bone; metaphyseal vessels predispose in children.
  • Compartment syndrome: Tight fascial compartments of limbs; urgent fasciotomy.
  • Arthritis: Osteoarthritis of weight-bearing joints; rheumatoid arthritis affects synovium.
  • Joint replacement: Hip/knee arthroplasty precautions and DVT prevention.
  • Pediatric: Epiphyseal plate injuries (Salter-Harris) affect growth.
  • Exam points: Anatomical snuffbox tenderness—scaphoid; anatomical landmarks for injections (e.g., ventrogluteal site).
  • Nursing relevance: Immobilization principles, neurovascular checks (pain, pallor, pulselessness, paresthesia, paralysis, poikilothermia).

LEVEL 4 — HAND NOTES

  • 206 bones in adult; ~270 in newborn.
  • 7 cervical, 12 thoracic, 5 lumbar; sacrum (5 fused), coccyx (3–5 fused).
  • True ribs 1–7; floating ribs 11–12.
  • Skull sutures: coronal, sagittal, lambdoid, squamous.
  • Longest bone: femur; smallest bone: stapes (ear).
  • Periosteum = pain sensitive; Endosteum lines medullary cavity.
  • Synovial joint mnemonic: PB H PCS B (Plane, Ball-socket, Hinge, Pivot, Condylar, Saddle, Ball) [remember two “Ball” means ball-socket only once—don’t double count in exam!]
  • Bone cells: Osteoblast builds; Osteoclast chews.
  • Epiphyseal plate = hyaline cartilage.
  • Important numbers: normal adult Ca2+ ~8.5–10.5 mg/dL (relevant to bone metabolism).
  • Exam trap: Teres major is NOT a rotator cuff muscle.

LEVEL 5 — HIGH-YIELD B.Sc Nursing

  • Axial vs appendicular classification and examples.
  • Vertebral features distinguishing cervical/thoracic/lumbar.
  • Blood supply of long bone—nutrient artery importance in fracture healing.
  • Common fracture patterns and neurovascular risks (humerus surgical neck—axillary nerve).
  • Synovial joint subtypes and examples (e.g., atlantoaxial—pivot).
  • Intervertebral disc = secondary cartilaginous (symphysis).
  • Scaphoid fracture avascular necrosis risk due to retrograde blood supply.
  • Medial circumflex femoral artery—chief supply to femoral head in adults.
  • Menisci are fibrocartilage—avascular in inner thirds.
  • Periosteum crucial for appositional growth and repair.
